As a study for reporting on travel, I'll introduce the route from Okayama Station to Okayama Castle.<br>
Many people go by streetcar, but it's a walkable distance. About 20-30 minutes.<br>
A bit less than 2 kilometers? (Google Maps route search shows 1.8km)<br>
JR Okayama Station. The Shinkansen and conventional lines are in the same station. The station facility "Sansute" is well-equipped. Convenient for travelers for souvenirs and meals.<br>
There's also a supermarket, Muji, bookstore, etc., and I think many people use it regularly.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6210/6098875422_970da652a4.jpg" alt="" />

Exiting the east exit, you can see Takashimaya on the right and Bic Camera on the left. (Incidentally, the west exit has ANA Hotel and a highway bus terminal.) The station square often has events. In the photo, it's small and hard to see, but there's a statue of Momotaro and his companions, and a fountain with an unusual shape. Someone uploaded a video.

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NGJ1Zt0dZ98" title="Okayama Station Square Fountain" target="_blank">Okayama Station Square Fountain (YouTube)</a>

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6081/6098338309_a3c388f3df.jpg" alt="" />

The streetcar stop at Okayama Station. There are 2 lines: "Higashiyama Line" and "Seikibashi Line." When going to the castle, take the "Higashiyama Line." The one in front is the Seikibashi Line with the new streetcar "MOMO." Okayama's streetcars have <a href="https://www.okayama-kido.co.jp/tramway/iroiro.html" title="various trains" target="_blank">various trains</a> like this.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6084/6098887360_92e759fd85.jpg" alt="" />

Momotaro-dori. Bic Camera on the left, streetcar on the right. Walk straight on this Momotaro-dori to the castle.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6182/6098891542_74a6b60dd4.jpg" alt="" />

The sidewalk is wide and tiled, making it easy to walk. You should watch out for bicycles.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6194/6098577835_a44df52f43.jpg" alt="" />

Walking for a while, you cross Nishikawa Greenway Park, a park that continues along a river. There's a fountain on the left. On the opposite side after crossing the road, there's a police box.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6068/6098579711_2810ce78e2.jpg" alt="" />

You come to a large intersection called Yanagawa. About halfway between the castle and the station.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6077/6099128914_6a5bf1170d.jpg" alt="" />

Approaching the streetcar "Joka" stop. There's an underground parking lot, and you can see <a href="https://www.okayama-symphonyhall.or.jp/" title="Okayama Symphony Hall" target="_blank">Okayama Symphony Hall</a> with a lavender-colored lower structure.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6197/6098582883_c6372dffe3.jpg" alt="" />

On the left is "Tenjin Ramen," an old-fashioned ramen shop typical of Okayama. I haven't eaten there yet. It seems to be open until around 2:30 PM, so there aren't many chances to eat.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6197/6099132258_0e9062e788.jpg" alt="" />

The streetcar "Joka" stop and Okayama Symphony Hall. If you take the streetcar to the castle, get off at this stop. I think it was 100 yen from the station.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6188/6098588063_648a240720.jpg" alt="" />

A slight slope from Joka to the castle. There's a stone monument saying "Crow Castle Road." Okayama Castle is also called "Crow Castle" (Ujo).

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6207/6098592405_177c0b408e.jpg" alt="" />

Going up "Crow Castle Road" a bit, you come to a park-like entrance with Korakuen on the left and the castle on the right. Just a bit more.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6090/6099141654_4295dbdfb5.jpg" alt="" />

The moat is the Asahi River. Crossing the bridge over that river leads to Korakuen.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6063/6098595279_2150a582a9.jpg" alt="" />

It's characterized by black lacquered weatherboards. So the overall impression is of a black castle. This castle also seems to have burned in the war, so it's made of concrete.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6086/6098596621_10d3995863.jpg" alt="" />

The stone walls are beautiful because islands in the Seto Inland Sea, which is a source of stone, were territories or nearby.

<img src="https://farm7.static.flickr.com/6201/6098599393_055c432747.jpg" alt="" />

The beauty of the stone walls is recommended. Also, it's good that the castle isn't over-maintained. Of course, Korakuen can be enjoyed in each season, so I definitely want to visit in autumn too.
